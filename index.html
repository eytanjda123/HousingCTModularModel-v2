            Inter-city comparison

            Same building, three cities

            Each bar is the building's total development cost and who pays for it. The grey and purple segments up to the dashed line are the gross sales price and how buyers pay it (own financing, Time To Own, the DOH homebuyer subsidy, and any remaining buyer gap in light orange). Past the dashed line, blue, teal and amber segments are developer-side money, filled in order: HDF, AHP (when counted), HTCC, the DOH rental subsidy and NMTC (when on), then DOH up to its cap. Red is the remaining developer gap. The dashed line is the appraised value, and triangles mark the baseline.

            Programs: what the current sources say, our leaps, and open questions (reviewed Nov 3, 2026)

            Each program is checked against its current primary source. "Leap" is what the model assumes beyond the source; "Check now" tests the current scenario against the program's rules, which stay notes, not hard limits. Unchecking a source removes it from every city's stack, to test a "what if this doesn't come through" case.

            Appraisal cases ·

            Four starting values for what the home appraises at

            The appraisal sets the sales price, and nobody knows yet what a new modular two-family will appraise for, so pick a starting value here or with Appraisal basis. The low case uses the median sale of existing 2-unit homes (CAMA). The base case ($340K) scales the $420K new-build value in the redacted New Haven DOH application down to our smaller home, and matches the 2024–25 resales of new two-families in Hartford ($133–$151 per sq ft). The DOH precedent is the $420K value itself, and the land bank value is the New Haven Land Bank's $429K for a new 2,400 sq ft two-family. A higher value shrinks the developer gap but raises the sales gap, property tax and maintenance reserve. Every other input stays as you've set it. The basis selector on the left sets which case drives the rest of the page.

            Building designs · focus city

            How Reframe's standard designs compare

            Each column runs the whole model with one building swapped in: units, bedrooms, sq ft and gross-up change, and every other input stays as you've set it. Pick a design with Building design at the top of the inputs to make it drive the page. Read the gaps with care: the appraisal stays at the value set for one two-family, so the bigger buildings show larger gaps than an appraiser would. Set the appraisal for each design before comparing them.

            Buyer analysis · · kept apart from the developer budget

            What the buyer can pay

            The developer budget counts the full gross sales price. This panel works out how the buyer pays it: their own mortgage and down payment, then Time To Own and the DOH homebuyer subsidy. Whatever is left is the remaining buyer gap, which is never added to the developer gap.

            How it's worked out: income, rent, rate and buyer subsidies (editable)

            Homeowner ·

            Can the buyer sustain ownership?

            The same building and appraised value at four income targets. Costs include taxes, insurance, energy, common utilities and a maintenance reserve. The income left over is compared with the MIT living-wage budget for the selected household. The stress test hits the owner with a 3-month vacancy, a 20% tax reassessment and a $10K repair at once. The highlighted column is the current income target.

            Method comparison ·

            Volumetric modular vs. the stick-built baseline, line by line

            Kate's DOH table, live. Every line uses the land bank's base-average cost structure. For volumetric, each line shows what a local contractor still does; the factory price covers the rest. Both use the same site work, foundation, soft costs and sales price. The building is faster to deliver as modular, so the construction loan is open for fewer months.

            Add-on features and procurement ·

            What each add-on costs the project and saves the owner

            Every add-on starts switched off, so the base model still matches the workbook. Each row shows what that feature would do on its own, with the rest of your current settings. The project pays the upfront cost, which raises the developer gap. The owner keeps the monthly savings. Turn features on and set their details in the panel on the left, under "Add-on features and procurement".

            Cost-saving levers ·

            The land bank's measures, one at a time and together

            The New Haven Land Bank's webinar lists measures that cut its gap from $390K to $242K per home. Each row switches one on by itself, with everything else as you've set it; the last row switches them all on. Switch them on for real under Cost-saving levers in the inputs.

            Market data · New Haven

            Who is short of homes: the RKG housing study

            From the land bank's RKG Associates study (HUD income limits, ACS 2022, RKG calculations). The ownership shortage is above 100% AMI, which backs the 100–120% AMI buyer target; at 50–80% AMI there's a surplus because higher earners buy down.

            Portfolio and mixed income

            A phase of buildings across cities and incomes

            Each row is a group of buildings in one city at one owner income target, tagged with its neighborhood and whether it's in a Qualified Census Tract. The default phase is the land bank's 6-home model (12 units) in New Haven. The developer gap per building doesn't change with income when a homebuyer subsidy fills the sales gap, but the buyer-side money does. Totals are checked against the caps the program documents state. The starting rows are an example only.

            The sweep follows the last slider you touched and runs across its researched range, with every other input held where it is. A city-only input is set to the same value in all three cities. Dots mark each city's current value.
